Twelve questions we need ruled on

Each of these is a point where two traditions both have a claim. We have chosen a default so the app works today; we need to know whether to keep it. Where the answer differs for Nepal and for India, please say so — the app already knows which country a user is in.

Panchang

1. Ayanamsa. We use Lahiri (Chitrapaksha), currently about 24°12′. Some Nepali almanacs use a slightly different value, and Raman and Krishnamurti ayanamsas are in use elsewhere. Should Lahiri stand for both countries? A difference of even a few arc-minutes can move a tithi ending by several minutes.

2. Sunrise definition. We take sunrise as the moment the upper limb of the Sun clears the horizon, corrected for refraction. The alternative is the centre of the disc, which falls roughly two minutes later. Every Choghadiya, hora and kaal window shifts with this choice. Which does your almanac use?

Your answer:

3. Whose sunrise. Today every Nepali user sees Kathmandu's sunrise and every Indian user sees New Delhi's. We can move to the user's own location. But then a family in Pokhara and one in Kathmandu would see Rahu Kaal at different times, and their printed panchang says Kathmandu. Which is right for this app — the user's place, or the country's reference city?

4. Amanta or purnimanta. We compute both and display purnimanta. What should each country default to, and should the user be allowed to change it?

Your answer:

5. Abhijit muhurta. We place it as the eighth of the fifteen muhurtas of the daylight. Several almanacs instead give local noon ± 24 minutes, which is close but not identical. Which should we publish? And is Abhijit to be omitted on Wednesdays, as some traditions hold?

6. Choghadiya quality. We mark Char, Labh, Amrit and Shubh auspicious and Udveg, Kaal and Rog inauspicious. Some almanacs treat Char as "variable" rather than plainly good. Should we show a third, middle category?

Your answer:

7. Hora quality. We mark Guru, Shukra, Budha and Chandra auspicious, Surya fair, and Shani and Mangal inauspicious. Is that the classification you would put in front of a user, or should hora be shown with the lord alone and no judgement attached?

8. Gulika and Mandi. We label the window "Gulika Kaal". Some traditions distinguish Gulika from Mandi and compute them differently. Are they one thing for our purposes?

Charts and matching

10. House system. The birth chart uses whole-sign houses. Should it stay whole-sign, or move to Sripati or Placidus cusps — and does the answer differ between the North and South chart styles we draw?

Your answer:

11. Unknown birth time. When someone does not know their birth time we refuse to show the lagna, the houses, and anything built on them, and we say why. The alternative many apps take is a silent sunrise chart. Is refusing correct, or should we offer a sunrise chart clearly labelled as such?

Your answer:

12. Matching, and what we are allowed to say. Ashtakoota gives a number out of 36. We currently show the number with a short verdict and a line saying it is one input among many, not advice about a marriage. Is that the right restraint? And should a Nadi or Bhakoot failure be shown as an outright blocker, or as one factor with its cancellations listed?

Your answer:

Regional panchang

We intend to offer Tamil, Telugu and Malayalam panchang. Our understanding is that these are largely the same computation under different conventions — solar versus lunar month reckoning, different era labels and month names, and regional additions such as Gowri Nalla Neram.

If that is wrong, this is the most useful correction you can give us. Please describe what genuinely differs in each tradition beyond naming, and whether a single engine can serve all of them honestly.
